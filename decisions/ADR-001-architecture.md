# Architecture Quality Requirements and Alternatives Analysis

This document records the architectural evaluation, selected capability structure, and trade-offs mapped against our project's core quality requirements for the Logistics-Route-Decider-System.

| Quality Requirement | Selected Architecture | Why Chosen & Advantages | Disadvantages & Risks | Alternatives Evaluated |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Scalability & Planning Efficiency**<br>(Support 50 concurrent users and optimize batches up to 100 orders within 15 minutes) | Capability-Oriented Modular Monolith | **Why Chosen:** Processes complex optimization algorithms in-memory between subsystems with minimal overhead (<10ms), easily meeting the 15-minute planning target without network lag.<br><br>**Advantages:** Single deployment artifact, fast execution, simple setup, and high cohesion within capability clusters. | **Disadvantages:** Relies heavily on developer discipline and automated CI checks to prevent module code leakage and maintain clean domain boundaries. | **Traditional Layered Monolith:** Tends to bottleneck under heavy dataset processing due to tight coupling between logic and data access.<br><br>**Event-Driven Microservices:** Introduces unnecessary network serialization overhead and operational complexity for 50 concurrent users. |
| **Data Integrity**<br>(100% preservation of order details, vehicle assignment, and route state without duplication or loss) | Capability-Oriented Modular Monolith | **Why Chosen:** Enforces strict domain capability boundaries while leveraging robust local relational database transactions, ensuring atomic updates for completed order and route states.<br><br>**Advantages:** Strong data consistency out-of-the-box, straightforward debugging, and simplified database failover management. | **Disadvantages:** Database scaling is tied to a single primary instance with read-replicas, rather than independent service-level database sharding. | **Traditional Layered Monolith:** Blurs data boundaries across horizontal layers over time, increasing the risk of race conditions and orphaned records.<br><br>**Event-Driven Microservices:** Requires complex distributed transactions (such as Sagas or two-phase commits) across decentralized data stores, creating high vulnerability to data drift. |
